Kangmin Cho

Graduate Researcher in Systems Engineering • Cornell University

I am a computational energy systems researcher working on how machine learning and scenario modeling can make uncertainty explicit in energy transition decisions. My work covers from renewable supply and hydrogen system design, to state-level grid planning and the verification of sustainability claims. In particular, my core work includes deep generative modeling of renewable power.

Previously, I conducted circular economy and sustainability assessment research on computing hardware and semiconductor materials at the Commodity Ecology Research Lab (Stony Brook University, SUNY Korea).

Supply-Side Renewables Uncertainty • Co-First Author

Generative Scenarios for Green Hydrogen Under Renewable Uncertainty

Techno-economic and life cycle assessments of power-to-X systems typically use a single historical year or a Typical Meteorological Year (TMY), which hides interannual variability. I led the cGAN component of a cGAN-MILP framework that generates synthetic 24-hour wind and solar profiles conditioned on temperature, wind speed, and irradiance. It is trained on 10 years of hourly West Texas data (87,673 observations, 2015-2024). The architecture combines two-layer stacked LSTMs with residual connections, a zero-gate and power-predictor factorization ($\hat{P}^i(t) = \phi_{gate}^i \cdot \phi_{predictor}^i$) that represents exact zeros, and a four-way pooled discriminator. A post-hoc correction enforces turbine cut-in ($3\text{ m/s}$) and cut-out ($22\text{ m/s}$) limits.

My contributions include extending the training record to a decade, revising the output layers and activation functions, hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, 30-seed reproducibility runs, and the validation figures. Across 30 independently seeded runs, held-out $R^2$ was $0.90 \pm 0.08$ (PV) and $0.83 \pm 0.14$ (wind). The ensemble median tracks the TMY while the 5th-95th percentile band exposes interannual spread a single year cannot show. PV distributions matched closely ($\text{KS} = 0.275$), wind agreement was weaker ($\text{KS} = 0.275$). Propagating the ensemble through a two-phase $\epsilon$-constraint MILP developed by my co-author, $\text{LCOH}$ stayed tight ($0.92\text{–}0.97\text{ USD/kg H}_2$) while GWP spanned $1.56\text{-}1.78 \text{ CO}_2\text{eq./kg} \text{ H}_2$ and the optimal solar fraction varied by 35.6% (coefficient of variation), showing that single-profile analyses can understate design variability.

Regional Grid & Policy Planning • Course Project

NYS Grid Decarbonization Under Emerging Load

New York's CLCPA sets a 70% renewable electricity target for 2030 and 60% reduction of GHG emissions by 2040 (from 1990 levels). Large data center loads, building heat pumps, and EVs may strain both.

I am co-designing a LEAP scenario study asking whether the mandates remain feasible. Inputs include NYISO zonal loads and queues, the NYSERDA renewable pipeline, NYC Local Law 84 building benchmarking, EIA end-use demand and fuel prices, and NREL ATB costs. Five scenarios (Baseline, Data Center Growth, High Electrification, Accelerated Renewables, and Integrated Mitigation) will be compared on generation mix, winter reserve marin, 100-year GWP, and marginal cost of data center integration.

Institutional Governance • First Author

AI-Enabled Sustainability Auditing: A Dual-Corpus RAG-LLM Framework in the Energy Sector

Manual ESG assurance is slow and prone to inter-rater variance, while standalone LLMs hallucinate in ways that undermine audit traceability. I led the design of a six-layer, dual-corpus Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework that audits sustainability reports against the GRI 11 Oil and Gas Sector Standard, and directed its implementation with a team of M.Eng students. The pipeline parses PDFs with Docling, embeds 256-token sentence chunks with stella_en_1.5B_v5, and expands retrieved chunks with adaptive neighboring context.

Scoring uses an evidence-constrained 0–5 rubric, and a deterministic validator zeroes any score without valid citations. Evaluation combines retrieval and generation metrics (Recall, Effective Information Rate, Completeness, Hallucination, Irrelevancy). The target corpus is 732 reports from 99 energy firms (2014-2023).

Spatial Analysis • Class Project

Environmental Audit of Shale Gas Infrastructure in Pennsylvania

I used Google Earth Engine to build summer Landsat composites for 2003-05 and 2023-25 and computed vegetation ($\Delta\text{NDVI}$) and land surface temperature ($\Delta\text{LST}$) change.

I linked these to 6,455 unconventional wells and 127 pipelines across Washington, Bradford, and Susquehanna counties using QGIS, ArcGIS Pro, with USGS NLCD 2021 binary forest masks and 60m concentric Euclidean buffers (0–300m). Within 60m of well pads, mean $\Delta\text{NDVI}$ fell to about $-0.21\text{ to }-0.24$). Recovery with distance was faster in the legacy county than in newer drilling areas.
