Kangmin Cho

Graduate Researcher in Systems Engineering • Cornell University

I am a computational energy systems researcher investigating the intersection of machine learning, stochastic uncertainty modeling, and macro-scale energy policy evaluation. My research combines deep generative time-series modeling, retrieval-augmented language models, geospatial remote sensing, and regional grid simulation to design physically resilient and institutionally verifiable decarbonization pathways.

Previously, I conducted circular economy and sustainability assessment research on computing hardware and semiconductor materials at the Commodity Ecology Research Lab (Stony Brook University / SUNY Korea).

Supply-Side Renewables Uncertainty • Co-First Author

Physics-Informed Generative Modeling for Green Hydrogen Systems Under Renewable Uncertainty

Conventional techno-economic and life cycle assessments of power-to-X systems rely on single historical years or Typical Meteorological Year (TMY) baselines that smooth out interannual weather anomalies. I developed a conditional Generative Adversarial Network (cGAN) trained on 10 years of hourly meteorological data (87,673 observations, 2015–2024) in West Texas to synthesize 24-hour wind and solar power ensembles. The architecture integrates two-layer stacked LSTMs with residual connections, a factorized zero-gate and power-predictor branch ($\hat{P}^i(t) = \phi_{gate}^i \cdot \phi_{predictor}^i$), a four-way pooled discriminator, and post-hoc range clamping for wind turbine cut-in ($3\text{ m/s}$) and cut-out ($22\text{ m/s}$) thresholds.

Across 30 independent runs, the cGAN achieved high predictive accuracy ($R^2 = 0.966$ for PV; $0.931$ for wind) and outperformed the Sandia TMY baseline in wind power Wasserstein distance ($30.440$ vs. $31.856$). Propagating the 30 synthetic annual scenarios through a two-phase $\epsilon$-constraint MILP optimization revealed that while $\text{LCOH}$ ($0.92\text{–}0.97\text{ USD/kg H}_2$) remained tight, optimal solar sizing varied by 35.6%—exposing substantial sizing risks masked by deterministic TMY planning.

Regional Grid & Policy Planning • Co-Lead Researcher

NYS Grid Decarbonization Under Emerging Load: LEAP Scenario Assessment of CLCPA Feasibility

New York State's Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (CLCPA) mandates 70% renewable electricity by 2030 and a 60% emissions reduction by 2040. However, rapid growth in inflexible 24/7 data center loads is colliding with building heat pump and electric vehicle adoption, threatening both clean energy mandates and winter peak reliability.

I co-designed this macro-energy scenario modeling study in LEAP (base year 2023), integrating NYISO zonal load baselines and interconnection queues, NYSERDA renewable pipelines, NYC Local Law 84 monthly building benchmarking, U.S. EIA sector fuel trajectories, and NREL ATB technology cost curves. Across five scenarios (Baseline, Data Center Growth, High Electrification, Accelerated Renewables, and Integrated Mitigation), the model evaluates generation mix shifts, winter installed reserve margins, 100-year GWP, and the marginal system cost of data center integration.

Institutional Governance • First Author & System Architect

AI-Enabled Sustainability Auditing: A Dual-Corpus RAG-LLM Framework in the Energy Sector

Manual ESG assurance is slow and vulnerable to inter-rater variance, whereas standalone LLMs suffer from parametric hallucinations that undermine audit traceability. As first author, I architected a six-layer, dual-corpus Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework to audit 732 sustainability reports from 99 global oil and gas firms (2014–2023) against 16 qualitative indicators in the GRI 11 Oil and Gas Sector Standard. The system parses PDFs into hierarchical JSON via Docling, embeds 256-token sentence chunks (20-token overlap) using stella_en_1.5B_v5, and performs adaptive neighbor context expansion ($E_i = \{c_{i-w}, \dots, c_{i+w}\}$).

To guarantee verifiable attribution, I formulated an evidence-constrained 0–5 ordinal scoring specification paired with a deterministic post-generation citation validator that zeroes out unsupported claims, benchmarked via Recall@K, MRR@K, LLM-as-a-Judge (Relevance, Faithfulness, Alignment), and Negative Rejection Rate. Empirical audits expose widespread "rhetorical decoupling" between corporate climate narratives and verifiable KPIs.

Spatial & Physical Ground-Truthing • Sole Author

Environmental Audit of Shale Gas Infrastructure: A Multi-County Study in Pennsylvania

Complementing corporate text auditing with physical ground-truthing, I conducted a spatiotemporal remote sensing audit of unconventional Marcellus Shale natural gas infrastructure across Washington, Bradford, and Susquehanna counties in Pennsylvania. Using Google Earth Engine, I processed a 23-year summer baseline (2003–2025) of 30m Landsat 5/9 surface reflectance and thermal imagery to compute long-term vegetation ($\Delta\text{NDVI}$) and land surface temperature ($\Delta\text{LST}$) shifts.

Integrating geospatial records for 6,455 unconventional wells and 127 local pipelines in QGIS and ArcGIS Pro with USGS NLCD 2021 binary forest masks and 60m concentric Euclidean buffers (0–300m), the analysis revealed acute forest degradation within 60m of well pads ($\Delta\text{NDVI}$ of $-0.21\text{ to }-0.24$) and contrasting logarithmic recovery trajectories between legacy production zones and newer drilling frontiers.
